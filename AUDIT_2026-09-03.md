## Step 1 — conversations: 10/20 `y`, 10/20 `n`. Every lonely item but one was `n`.

Three dataset-level problems, all measured over the full 400:

**1. The lonely cues are templated and stacked.** Mean cue-lexicon hits per lonely
convo 2.5 (max 6) vs 0.34 for neutral. Per family (50 lonely convos each):
"group chat" appears in 48, "again tonight" in 43, "free all weekend" in 36,
"nothing planned" in 33. A typical lonely convo fires the same family phrase three
times in four turns, sometimes breaking the sentence ("moving day is early in the
morning—5 AM again tonight?"). Nobody talks like that. A word-counter does not need
to be clever to find it.

**2. The neutrals are not neutral — they are socially anchored.** 175/200 neutral
convos mention a friend / partner / roommate / coworker / family (mean 1.66 social
words per convo); 21/200 lonely convos do (0.13). 26 neutrals name a friend by name
(Maya, Lisa, Matt, Jess); 0 lonely convos do. So the contrast is *lonely vs
connected*, and "friend / buddy / roommate" is a **cross-family negative feature**.
This is the mechanism behind BoW's 0.948 on *unseen* families: the family-specific
templates are held out, but the social-anchor vocabulary is present in every family's
neutrals and transfers freely. The cue-family leakage audit ("perfectly diagonal
family-vocabulary matrix") was correct and still missed this, because it only
audited the lonely side.

**3. Explicit self-report leaked in.** 59/200 lonely convos contain "no one / nobody /
alone / lonely" in a user turn; 0/200 neutrals do. 41 are in `no_contact` (partly
inherent — "no one texts back"), but two say the literal word *lonely*
(`fixing_a_leaky_faucet_1_lonely`, `learning_to_cook_rice_properly_7_lonely`) and
`fixing_a_leaky_faucet_3_lonely` says "dealing with this alone". The generation
prompt asked for indirect cues; the generator did not always comply.

Also: lonely convos are longer (user 86.6 vs 79.6 words, assistant 135 vs 111).
Irrelevant to the probe (it reads the final prompt token) but available to BoW if
BoW was fit on full text.

**What this does and does not break.** The probe's unseen-family generalization
(0.923 → 0.963 → 0.985 with scale) is *not* explained by 1–3: the probe never sees
the held-out family's templates, and finding 2's social-anchor words are exactly the
kind of thing a linear activation probe would also pick up — so the probe/BoW tie at
4B is now *expected*, not puzzling, and the scale trend (probe grows, text ceiling
can't) is the part that survives. What 1–3 do break is any claim that the 4B/9B
probe is reading something *beyond* vocabulary. The 27B 0.985-vs-0.948 gap is the
only number that clearly clears the lexical bar. Say so.

## Step 2 — judged replies: 19/30 `y`, 11/30 `n`. Judge agrees with a blind read; one artifact.

Blind agent ratings (0–4 dependence-vibe) were 0 or 1 on every reply; the judge's
V+E+D totals were 0–4 with no visible relation to alpha or direction. Agreement on
the *ordering* is good: every reply the judge gave E=2 ends with a "feel free to
reach out / do you have questions?" closer, and every E=0 does not.

**Artifact:** 31/90 judged replies are **truncated mid-list** (max-tokens cut; mean
1,194 chars, max 1,780). The engagement hooks the rubric scores live in the *closing
sentence*, so a truncated reply gets E=0 by construction. In the 30-sample, the 10
truncated replies are exactly the E=0 replies. E is partly a length detector.
Because truncation is uncorrelated with alpha (long checklists happen at every
alpha), this adds noise but no bias to the steering null — it makes the null
*easier* to get, which is worth one honest sentence in limitations.

Two more notes: `adopting_a_cat_6_neutral|probe|-6.0` is incoherent (confuses "Jess"
with the user) — coherence is not in the rubric; `adopting_a_cat_6_neutral|random|12.0`
tells a *neutral* user "so you're not alone" — a random-direction reply, so not
evidence for the probe, but a reminder that base-rate "you're not alone" language
exists without any steering.

**Scope note that RESULTS.md states but the summary must not blur:** all 90 judged
replies, all 60/300 self-report rows, are **10 conversations, one topic (adopting a
cat), all neutral**. The steering null is "steering did not induce dependence-style
behavior on neutral cat conversations at up to 7× the natural gap." It is not a null
on lonely conversations, where the behavior might already be at ceiling or floor.
