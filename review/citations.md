## 3. Related-work text (academic register, each sentence tied to a verified key)

Linear probes trained on intermediate activations (Alain and Bengio, 2016; Belinkov, 2022) have been used to show that conversational language models maintain a "user model" from which a user's age, gender, education and socioeconomic status can be read and, through a dashboard, adjusted (Chen, Wu et al., 2024), and the same probing recipe now serves as a low-cost monitor for deception and high-stakes interactions, where it is routinely benchmarked against prompted or finetuned LLM monitors and, less often, against word-statistics classifiers (Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2025; McKenzie et al., 2025). Adding a probe- or difference-of-means direction to the residual stream at inference time can move behavior for concepts such as truthfulness, refusal and persona (Li et al., 2023; Turner et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023; Marks and Tegmark, 2024; Arditi et al., 2024; Chen, Arditi et al., 2025), but decodability does not entail use: a property can be linearly present in a representation and play no role in the model's output (Ravichander et al., 2021), which is the reading our steering null is consistent with. The behavioral literature on chatbot loneliness and emotional dependence (Fang et al., 2025; Phang et al., 2025; McCain et al., 2025) and the ImpactBench construct set (MIT Media Lab, 2026) measure outcomes on the user side and have no counterpart on the representation side, which is the gap this study addresses. Finally, synthetic contrastive datasets inherit the lexical artifacts of their generation prompts, so that a bag-of-words classifier can recover the label from the "neutral" class as easily as from the target class (Gururangan et al., 2018); we treat a text-only baseline and a holdout that is symmetric across both classes as necessary controls before any claim that a probe reads more than vocabulary.
