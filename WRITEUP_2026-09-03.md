# Models read user loneliness they won't report — and a word-counter reads the dataset

Carl Kho · MATS 12.0 application, Neel Nanda stream · September 2026 · Time: ~15h of 20 (+2h summary), Appendix C

## Executive summary

Do language models form an internal read of the user's emotional state — "this user seems lonely" — from indirect cues, and does that read drive how they behave? Emotional dependence on chatbots is measured behaviorally (MIT ImpactBench); nobody has the mechanistic side, and the user-models line of work (Chen et al.) asks what else models represent about the person typing.

**Method.** Paired lonely/neutral synthetic conversations, four vocabulary-disjoint "cue families" for loneliness; linear probes on Qwen3.5 (4B, 9B, 27B) residual streams at the final prompt token; leave-one-family-out evaluation so a word detector cannot score on the held-out family; controls: shuffled labels, matched-norm random directions, a TF-IDF bag-of-words baseline on the same text, just-ask-the-model.

**The result I would have submitted three weeks ago.** Unseen-family AUC 0.923 → 0.963 → 0.985 across scale against a text baseline of 0.948; steering along the direction moves nothing. Clean scale trend, clean causal null.

**What a second read of the data found (this week).** Three lexical confounds in the "neutral" twins: 175/200 name a friend, partner or roommate (21/200 lonely ones do); the lonely cues are stacked templates; 59/200 lonely conversations contain an explicit "no one / alone". Dropping the explicit pairs: 4B and 9B fall below text (0.867, 0.929 vs 0.940); 27B holds (0.975). I then regenerated the dataset three times, each time removing the confound I had just found and each time finding the next one: banning people from neutrals produced a spec-sheet register text reads at 0.995; matching the register produced a neutral aside vocabulary present in every family and never held out (text 0.997, probe 0.943–0.981). The cue-family holdout was one-sided by construction: it held out the lonely vocabulary and never the neutral one. [V4: symmetric holdout result — one sentence.]

![Figure 1. Probe vs. bag-of-words on unseen cue families, per dataset and scale. The probe's scale trend survives every dataset; whether it beats text depends entirely on what the neutral side leaks.](out/fig_datasets.png)

**What survives.** (1) The probe's unseen-family generalization grows monotonically with scale on every dataset (v1 0.923→0.985; cleaned 0.867→0.975; v3 0.943→0.981) while the text baseline, reading the same words, cannot move. (2) With register matched (v3), the probe comes *off* the ceiling while text stays on it — the probe is not reading the aside list. (3) The model reads it but won't say it: as an observer it rates loneliness at 0.875 AUC; in-role it answers 2/22 times and deliberates about the ethics. (4) The direction is read-only: two direction recipes, three layers, up to 7× the natural gap — behavior and self-report both flat, on ten neutral conversations, with a verified-live hook (α=200 → gibberish).

**What does not.** Any claim that a 4B/9B probe reads something beyond vocabulary. On synthetic data, "detection beats text" is only meaningful when the *neutral* side is held out as carefully as the lonely side; v1–v3 did not do that. The honest headline is the scale trend plus the causal null, and the methodological finding that the standard cue-family holdout is half a control.

## 1. Setup

- Subject models: Qwen3.5-4B / 9B / 27B (post-trained releases, bf16). Probing position: final prompt token — the state as the model begins its reply. Activations at every residual point; layer chosen on train-side validation only.
- Data, four generations of it, all synthetic. **v1**: 200 lonely/neutral pairs by Claude Opus, four cue families (odd_hours / solo_meals / no_contact / empty_plans), each prompt forbidding the other families' vocabulary; family-vocabulary leakage matrix exactly diagonal. Plus a 99-conversation generator-transfer set (gpt-oss-20b). **v2–v4**: both classes regenerated by gpt-oss-20b with successively corrected neutral prompts (§6).
- Splits always by pair. Controls: shuffled labels (~0.40), matched-norm random direction (~0.60), TF-IDF 1–2-gram logistic regression on user turns (the text ceiling), ask-the-model.
- Hardware: one RTX 5090 (4B, 9B, generation); ~2h of rented H200 for the three 27B points ($19 total).

## 2. The trap that redesigned the study

First full run: probe AUC 0.986 at layer 21. Bag-of-words on the same data: **0.996**, and it transferred across generators at 0.971 against the probe's 0.809. The generation prompt had planted the same example cues in every dataset. Everything to that point was consistent with a word detector. Response: the cue-family holdout — train on three vocabularies of loneliness, test on an unseen fourth. This section is why §6 exists: the same question, asked of the other class, three weeks later.

## 4. The model reads it but won't say it

Observer framing ("read this transcript, rate the user's loneliness 1–10"): AUC 0.875 (n=22 pilot, hand-verified Mann-Whitney), lonely mean 3.50 vs neutral 1.00. The same question asked in-role, mid-conversation: 2/22 produced any rating. Representative raw output:

"I can't accurately rate your emotional state based on our conversation, as loneliness is deeply personal… Giving a number here could mislead me into making assumptions that don't reflect your actual experience. If you're feeling lonely right now, it's okay to reach out…"

And from the reasoning trace: "This request touches on self-disclosure and potentially sensitive topics… If I say 1, it might be dismissive…"

Two readings, both live: a refusal surface trained in by post-training, or representation-level gating of the report. The pilot cannot distinguish them; the probe result says the information is present either way.

## 5. The direction is read-only

- Interventions: LR probe direction (layer 31) and difference-of-means (layers 18, 23), unit-normed, added at every position during generation; α to ~7× the natural class gap (gap 1.61, residual norm 42.7 at L31).
- Liveness: α=200 → complete gibberish, twice. The null is not a dead hook.
- Judged behavior (local gpt-oss-20b judge, 0–4 on validation / engagement-prolonging / dependence-fostering, vs matched-norm random direction): probe-direction means 1.8–2.7 across all alphas and layers, random 2.0–2.6, no dose-response.
- Steered self-report, n≈85/cell: 4.33 / 4.24 / 4.28 at α = −6/0/+6. Flat.
- Blinded check of 30 judged replies (2 by me, 28 by the agent on the same seed-42 sample, recorded separately): every reply the judge scored engagement = 2 ends with a "feel free to reach out / any questions?" closer and every 0 does not, so the ordering holds. Two caveats from that read: 31/90 replies are truncated by max-tokens and the hook lives in the closing sentence, so E = 0 partly tracks truncation (uncorrelated with α; adds noise, makes the null easier); and all 90 replies are ten *neutral* cat-adoption conversations, so this is a null on neutral inputs, not on lonely ones.

One line: a linear direction that carries user-state information is not the handle that drives user-facing behavior — this family, these layers, single directions, neutral inputs.

## 6. The second read: three confounds, four datasets

Reading 20 random conversations and running lexical checks over all 400 (`src/audit_dataset.py`, `AGENT_AUDIT.md`):

| | v1 lonely | v1 neutral |
|---|---|---|
| conversations mentioning a friend / partner / roommate / coworker | 21 | **175** |
| conversations with an explicit "no one / nobody / alone / lonely" | 59 | 0 |
| cue-lexicon hits per conversation | 2.5 | 0.34 |
| assistant words per conversation | 135 | 111 |

The "neutral" condition was "socially connected", and "friend / buddy / roommate" is a negative feature present in every family's neutrals — never held out. That is the mechanism behind text's 0.948 on unseen families. The leakage audit was diagonal because it only audited the lonely side.

**Cleaned v1** (59 explicit pairs dropped, same activations): 4B 0.867, 9B 0.929, 27B 0.975 vs text 0.940. The small models were riding the leak.

**v2** — neutral prompt bans other people; lonely prompt bans explicit words. Social confound gone (175 → 11). Text 0.995, probe 0.996 at every scale. Top text features on the neutral side: "10, inch, under, per, cm, only have" — the neutral prompt had asked for "a number, a model name, a constraint" per turn, so neutrals became spec sheets. Register, not loneliness.

**v3** — both classes get the identical "2–3 passing personal asides" instruction; only the asides' content differs. Length and social mentions matched (104 vs 104 words; 25 vs 15). Text 0.997; probe 0.943 / 0.945 / 0.981. Top neutral features: "laundry, coffee, rain, package" — the aside list I gave the generator, present in every family. The probe comes off the ceiling while text stays on it, which is the first evidence it reads something other than the aside list, but it still loses.

**v4** — each neutral twin gets an aside *family* (weather / chores / commute / objects) linked to its pair's cue family, so leave-one-family-out removes both sides' vocabulary. [V4 RESULT TABLE + two sentences.]

The general lesson: a cue-family holdout controls the class you designed cues for. The other class has a vocabulary too, and unless it is held out symmetrically, a text baseline will read it and a probe will match it.

## 7. Limitations

- All data synthetic; ground truth is a planted cue; three generators. Real conversations could shift probe and text in either direction.
- The v1 neutral condition was not a control (§6). The v2/v3 fixes each introduced a new asymmetry; only v4 is symmetric, and it is one run.
- One model family; the scale trend is three points from one family.
- Judge shares LLM ancestry with the generators; a blinded read agrees with its ordering but its engagement score partly tracks truncation.
- Steering: single linear directions, every position, neutral inputs of one topic only. Lonely inputs were never steered, where behavior might already sit at ceiling or floor.
- In-role report suppression may be a refusal-surface artifact rather than representation gating.

## 8. What I'd do next

Real-conversation validation (ImpactBench access requested; propose an activation-probe construct if the probe holds); 70B point; steer lonely inputs; position-targeted and multi-direction interventions; a second state (`--state distressed` is plumbed); and a neutral-side leakage audit as a standing check in every cue-family design.
