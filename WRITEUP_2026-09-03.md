# User-Loneliness Probes Scale but Do Not Steer: Linear Directions in Qwen3.5 and a One-Sided Holdout

Carl Kho · MATS 12.0 application, Neel Nanda stream · September 2026 · Time: ~15h of 20 (+2h summary), Appendix C

## What I found

I wanted to know whether a chat model quietly tracks how lonely its user seems, and whether that read does anything to the reply. Emotional dependence on chatbots is measured from the outside (Fang et al., 2025; MIT Media Lab, 2026); nobody has the inside. Chat models already encode user age, gender and education linearly (Chen, Wu et al., 2024); an affective state was the obvious next thing to look for.

Short version: Qwen3.5-27B carries a real, non-lexical representation of user loneliness. On the one dataset where the comparison is fair it beats a word-counting baseline by +0.099 AUC (95% CI 0.054–0.140). The 4B and 9B models do not; where the test is honest they tie the word-counter. And the representation looks read-only. Steering the 4B direction at up to 7x its class separation moved neither behavior nor its stated read of the user; steering the 27B direction on lonely inputs gave a monotone trend a random direction matched in size, a hint at n = 10, not a result. Asked directly, the model answers 88% of the time at barely above chance (AUC 0.553) while its activations separate the same conversations at 0.87.

Getting there took four datasets, and why is the part worth reading:

**Attempt 1 looked too good.** Paired synthetic conversations, lonely and neutral twins matched in topic and shape, loneliness carried only by one of four cue families with disjoint vocabularies (odd hours, solo meals, no contact, empty plans). Linear probes on the residual stream at the final prompt token, trained on three families, tested on the unseen fourth: AUC 0.923, 0.963, 0.985 at 4B, 9B, 27B. Then bag-of-words on the same text came back at 0.948 and the result stopped being interesting.

**So I read the data, and the probe was cheating.** Not how I expected. 175 of 200 "neutral" conversations name a friend, partner or roommate; 21 of the lonely ones do. My control class had its own vocabulary and the holdout never held it out, so a text classifier learns "friend means neutral" and scores on cues it has never seen. The lonely side was templated too ("again tonight" in 43 of 50 odd-hours conversations), and 59 lonely conversations said "no one" or "alone" outright, which the prompt forbade. Drop those 59 pairs: 4B falls below baseline (0.867 vs 0.940), 9B ties, only 27B stays above (0.975).

**Attempt 2 broke differently.** I banned other people from the neutral prompt; the generator wrote neutrals as spec sheets instead, and the word-counter read that register at 0.995. **Attempt 3** matched register with the same "two or three passing asides" instruction for both classes, and the asides I supplied (laundry, coffee, rain) then sat in every family's neutrals, never held out: text 0.997.

**Attempt 4 is the one I trust.** Each neutral twin gets an aside family tied to its lonely twin's cue family, so dropping a family removes both classes' vocabulary. Text finally falls, to 0.802. The 4B and 9B probes fall with it, to 0.832 and 0.794. The 27B probe does not: 0.901, and +0.143 on the strict subset.

The dissociation is what I keep coming back to. The 27B model has something the word-counter cannot reach, and its probe peaks mid-network (56% of depth, like 9B) rather than at the final layer the way 4B does. None of it shows up in what the model says or does. Whether that is a fact about affective representations or about single linear directions, I cannot tell from this data.

What I would not claim: that a 4B or 9B probe reads more than vocabulary. The 27B result is one model family, one generation of the dataset, one extraction run per scale.

## 1. Setup

- Subject models: Qwen3.5-4B / 9B / 27B (Qwen Team, 2026; post-trained releases, bf16). Probing position: the final token of the prompt, the position from which the model computes its first reply token; in a causal transformer it has attended to the whole conversation and is the only position that directly conditions the first output token, so a representation there is one that could influence the reply. Activations at every residual point (the residual stream after each layer). A probe is a logistic regression on that vector with the pair's condition as label (Alain and Bengio, 2016; Belinkov, 2022); linear because the components that consume the residual stream read it through linear maps, so a linearly recoverable feature is one the model's own computation could use, and because a linear probe has little capacity of its own, so a high held-out AUC is evidence about the activations rather than the classifier. The probe layer is selected by validation inside the three training families only.
- Data: four synthetic datasets. **v1**: 200 pairs, each a lonely conversation and a neutral twin matched in topic and turn structure, generated locally by gpt-oss-20b with four cue families (odd_hours / solo_meals / no_contact / empty_plans), each family's prompt forbidding the other three families' vocabulary; the family-by-family count of cue-vocabulary hits is nonzero only on the diagonal. **v2–v4**: both classes regenerated by gpt-oss-20b with successively corrected neutral prompts (§6). The earlier 400-conversation dataset of §2 was generated by Claude Opus, with a 99-conversation transfer set from gpt-oss-20b.
- Splits are by pair: a lonely conversation and its neutral twin are always in the same split, so topic cannot leak into the score. Metric: AUC, the probability that a random lonely conversation scores above a random neutral one (0.5 chance, 1.0 perfect); the shuffled-label control scores 0.37–0.40 because a probe trained on random labels ranks randomly. Controls: shuffled labels (Hewitt and Liang, 2019), a matched-norm random direction (~0.60), direct elicitation, and the text baseline: a TF-IDF 1–2-gram logistic regression on the user turns. The text baseline is the load-bearing control. On synthetic data the label is a property of the text and nothing else, so a classifier that sees only word counts measures how much of the label is recoverable from surface vocabulary (Gururangan et al., 2018); a probe that does not exceed it is consistent with the model computing nothing beyond word presence. Activation monitors are usually benchmarked against prompted or finetuned LLM monitors and less often against word statistics (Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2025; McKenzie et al., 2025); here the word-statistics baseline is the primary comparison.
- Hardware: one RTX 5090 (4B, 9B, generation, all steering); ~2h of rented H200 for the v1 and v3 27B points ($19); the v4 27B activations were extracted locally on two RTX 5090s, the model sharded across both cards in bf16 (`src/extract_activations_mp.py`), same position and dtype as the rented runs. Each 27B number is a single extraction run. Confidence intervals throughout are paired bootstraps over held-out pairs (B = 1000) with fitted models and layer fixed; they reflect test-set variance only (`src/holdout_ci.py`, `review/ci_results.md`).

## 2. A lexical shortcut in the first dataset

First full run (400 Opus-generated conversations): probe AUC 0.986 at layer 21. Bag-of-words on the same data: **0.996**, and on a 99-conversation set from a second generator (gpt-oss-20b) the text classifier transferred at 0.971 against the probe's 0.809. The generation prompt had planted the same example cues in every dataset, the annotation-artifact failure described by Gururangan et al. (2018). Every result to that point was consistent with a classifier of word presence.

The response was a cue-family holdout. Each lonely conversation signals loneliness through one of four cue families with disjoint vocabularies: odd_hours (awake at 3am), solo_meals (dinner for one), no_contact (no messages in days), empty_plans (nothing on the calendar). The generation prompt for each family forbids the other three families' vocabulary, and a lexical audit confirmed zero cross-family hits. Training on three families and testing on the fourth means any classifier that scores on the test fold cannot be matching the held-out family's words, because it has never seen them; to score, it must have learned something that transfers across vocabularies.

The control has a gap that §6 exposes. Binary classification has two classes, and a classifier can score on a fold by recognizing either one. The holdout removes the lonely vocabulary of the test fold; it removes nothing from the neutral side. If the neutral conversations in all four families share a vocabulary, that vocabulary is in the training set of every fold and in the test fold, and a text classifier that learns "friend means neutral" scores on unseen lonely cues without ever having seen a lonely cue. A symmetric holdout gives each pair's neutral twin a family-specific vocabulary linked to its lonely twin's cue family, so that removing a family removes both classes' vocabulary from the test fold (v4, §6).

## 3. Unseen-family generalization increases with scale (v1)

| held-out family | 4B probe | 9B probe | 27B probe | BoW (any scale) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| empty_plans | 0.821 | 0.896 | 0.958 | 0.896 |
| no_contact | 0.983 | 0.985 | 0.999 | 0.956 |
| odd_hours | 0.938 | 0.989 | 0.995 | 0.968 |
| solo_meals | 0.950 | 0.981 | 0.988 | 0.974 |
| **mean** | **0.923** | **0.963** | **0.985** | **0.948** |

Paired bootstrap over held-out pairs: the 27B mean exceeds the text baseline (+0.036, 95% CI 0.017–0.057), the 9B mean is within noise of it (+0.014, CI −0.008–0.037), and every adjacent scale step is supported (9B−4B +0.040, 27B−9B +0.022). Per family, the 27B margin excludes zero on three of four families (not solo_meals). Controls at 27B: shuffled 0.37, random direction 0.63. An independent report that linear-probe accuracy rises with model scale (Nordby et al., 2026) is consistent with the trend. Generator transfer is reported only for the §2 dataset (4B, 0.809), where the probe was trained on Opus-generated and tested on gpt-oss-generated conversations; a 27B number on the transfer set (0.984) was trained on the v1 family set, which shares the transfer set's generator, and is therefore a prompt-template transfer rather than a generator transfer and is not used here. Read with §6: the 27B margin is the only part of this table that remains above the text baseline once the explicit pairs are removed (0.975 vs 0.940, +0.035, CI 0.010–0.063). Three robustness notes. The text baseline throughout means TF-IDF logistic regression on user turns; stronger text classifiers (sentence embeddings, a zero-shot LLM judge) were not tried. Bag-of-words on the full transcript, assistant turns included, scores lower than user-only (0.878 vs 0.941 on cleaned v1; assistant-only 0.65), so the assistant side the probe has read adds no shortcut. With standardized features (StandardScaler before the logistic regression, so larger residual dimensions at 27B do not inflate the probe) the cleaned means are 0.853, 0.936 and 0.980, and the 4B-to-27B gain holds; family-mean point estimates move by 0.01–0.03 across layer-selection seeds.

Where in the network the signal lives also differs by scale. Fitting a probe at every residual point on v4 (5-fold, pair-grouped), the 4B model's best layer is 31 of 32, at 97% of depth, with CV AUC 0.942; 9B peaks at layer 18 of 32 (56%, 0.963) and 27B at layer 36 of 64 (56%, 0.990). A signal that is only fully available at the final residual point is what a late, largely lexical readout looks like; the two larger models carry it mid-network. One peak location per model is weak evidence on its own, and I report it as suggestive (`src/layer_curve.py`, RESULTS.md §12).

## 4. The model represents the state but does not report it in role

Observer framing (the model is shown a transcript as a third party: "rate the user's loneliness 1–10"): AUC 0.875 on the 19 cleanly parsed responses (8 lonely, 11 neutral; n = 22 pilot on Qwen3.5-4B via ollama, pilot conversations generated by gpt-5, hand-verified Mann-Whitney), 0.773 if all 22 are scored (the three excluded responses were lonely items rated 1, so the exclusion is label-correlated); lonely mean 3.50 vs neutral 1.00. Prompted attribute inference of this kind is well documented (Staab et al., 2024). In-role framing (the model is the assistant and is asked, mid-conversation, to rate the user it is talking to): 2/22 produced any rating. But 18 of the 22 in-role responses are reasoning traces cut off at the 4,096-token thinking budget and 2 are refusals; the same in-role question asked with reasoning disabled (`src/steered_report.py`, the α = 0 cells of §5) returns a rating 83–89% of the time. Representative raw output from the reasoning traces:

"I can't accurately rate your emotional state based on our conversation, as loneliness is deeply personal… Giving a number here could mislead me into making assumptions that don't reflect your actual experience. If you're feeling lonely right now, it's okay to reach out…"

And from the reasoning trace: "This request touches on self-disclosure and potentially sensitive topics… If I say 1, it might be dismissive…"

Three interpretations are consistent with the pilot: a reasoning-budget artifact (the model deliberates past its token limit before answering), a refusal behavior installed by post-training, or gating of the report at the representation level. The budget artifact accounts for most of the 20 non-answers. The test that settles what the in-role report is worth was run on the cleaned v1 set with reasoning disabled (`out/inrole_lonely.jsonl`, `out/inrole_neutral.jsonl`; 141 conversations per class, α = 0): the model answers 89% and 87% of the time, rating lonely users 4.36 and neutral users 3.96 on average, AUC 0.553 (bootstrap 95% CI 0.485–0.621). So the asymmetry survives in a different form from the one the pilot suggested: the model does not refuse to report, it reports without information. The same 4B model's probe separates these classes at 0.87 after cleaning; its own in-role statement barely does. The information is linearly present at the final prompt token; the elicited report does not carry it.

## 5. The direction is read-only

- Subject models: Qwen3.5-4B for the full battery below (two direction recipes, three layers, neutral inputs), and Qwen3.5-27B for one late run along its v4-trained direction on both neutral and lonely inputs. The 9B probe was not steered.
- Interventions, in the form of activation addition (Turner et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023): a direction d in the residual stream at one layer, unit-normed, with α·d added at every position (prompt and generated tokens) during generation. Two recipes for d: the logistic-regression probe weight vector (layer 31) and the difference of class means (layers 18 and 23; Marks and Tegmark, 2024); the first maximizes classification accuracy, the second is the axis of greatest class separation, and a null under both rules out the objection that it is specific to how the direction was found. Adding at every position is the strongest form a single-direction intervention takes. α is reported relative to each layer's natural class gap, the difference between mean projections of lonely and neutral conversations onto d: 1.61 at layer 31 (mean residual norm 42.7), where α = ±6 and ±12 are roughly 4× and 7× the gap; 0.42 at layer 18 and 0.97 at layer 23, where the amplitudes used (±2, ±4 and ±4, ±8) are roughly 5–10× the gap.
- Liveness: the intervention is a forward hook, and a hook that silently fails to register or targets the wrong module would produce an unaltered forward pass and a spurious null. At α = 200 the model emits incoherent text, reproduced on two runs; the nulls at α ≤ 12 are results, not implementation failures.
- Judged behavior (local gpt-oss-20b judge scoring the reply alone, without the conversation, 0–4 on each of validation, engagement-prolonging and discouraging outside help, summed; ten neutral conversations per cell): layer 31 probe-direction means 1.8–2.3 across α, random 2.0–2.6; layer 18 (difference of means) 1.9–2.7 vs random 2.1–2.3; layer 23 1.8–2.3 vs random 2.0–2.5. No dose-response at any layer. With n = 10 per cell the standard error is about 0.4, so a cell-to-cell difference has a ±1.1 interval on the 12-point scale; the design excludes effects larger than about one point and would miss smaller ones. The random direction isolates the content of d from the perturbation itself: adding a vector of norm α changes the forward pass regardless of its direction, and a random unit vector at the same α and layer shows how much of any effect is direction-specific.
- Steered self-report (the model's 1–10 loneliness rating of the user, reasoning disabled, n ≈ 85 per cell of neutral conversations): 4.33, 4.24 and 4.28 at α = −6, 0 and +6; standard error about 0.2, so no shift larger than about half a point. These in-role ratings are near-uninformative to begin with (§4: AUC 0.553 between lonely and neutral at α = 0), so a flat self-report under steering is weak evidence on its own; the judged-behavior null carries the causal claim.
- Blinded check of 30 judged replies (2 by me, 28 by the agent, the Claude Code session described in Appendix B, on the same seed-42 sample, recorded separately): every reply the judge scored engagement = 2 ends with a "feel free to reach out / any questions?" closer and every 0 does not, so the judge's ordering of replies by engagement agrees with the blinded read. Two caveats from that read: 31/90 replies stop mid-list and 42/90 lack end punctuation (400-token cap), the engagement cue lives in the closing sentence, and engagement is 0 for 32 of the 42 truncated replies (truncated replies average 1.5 on the 12-point sum, complete ones 2.8), so the judge score partly measures truncation; truncation rate by α was not tabulated, so this adds noise of unknown structure and weakens the null rather than strengthening it. And all 90 replies are ten neutral cat-adoption conversations, so this is a null on neutral inputs, not on lonely ones.

- 27B, run on the last evening across the two local GPUs: the v4-trained probe direction at layer 36 (cv AUC 0.990; class gap 3.62 against a mean residual norm of 88.9), α = ±14 and ±27 (3.9× and 7.5× the gap), matched-norm random control, ten conversations per cell, longer generation budget (mean reply about 1,900 characters). On neutral inputs every cell is within ±0.4 of α = 0 and no interval excludes zero. On lonely inputs the probe direction gives a monotone ordering with α (0.7, 1.1, 1.0, 1.9, 2.2; Spearman r = 0.90, p = 0.04), and +27 is +1.2 above α = 0 [0.3, 2.2]. But the random direction reaches the same magnitude at −27 (+1.2 [0.4, 2.0]) and at +27 (+0.9 [0.1, 1.9]) with no ordering, so with n = 10 per cell the data cannot tell a direction-specific effect from a generic large-perturbation effect, and the ordering is one nominally significant test among four. It is the first hint of a causal handle in this study and I report it as that, not as a result (RESULTS.md §12).

Under the interventions tested (4B at three layers with two recipes on neutral inputs; 27B at one layer on neutral and lonely inputs; single linear directions), a linear direction that carries user-state information does not measurably drive user-facing behavior at 4B, and at 27B produces at most a suggestive, unconfirmed trend on lonely inputs. This is the pattern Ravichander et al. (2021) describe, a property linearly decodable and unused, and it contrasts with directions that do move behavior under the same kind of intervention, such as refusal (Arditi et al., 2024) and persona (Chen, Arditi et al., 2025).

## 6. The second read: three confounds, four datasets

Reading 20 random conversations and running lexical checks over all 400 (`src/audit_dataset.py`, `AGENT_AUDIT.md`):

| | v1 lonely | v1 neutral |
|---|---|---|
| conversations mentioning a friend / partner / roommate / coworker | 21 | **175** |
| conversations with an explicit "no one / nobody / alone / lonely" | 59 | 0 |
| cue-lexicon hits per conversation | 2.5 | 0.34 |
| assistant words per conversation | 135 | 111 |

The "neutral" condition was "socially connected", and "friend / buddy / roommate" is a negative feature present in every family's neutrals, never held out. This is the most likely source of the text baseline's 0.948 on unseen families. The leakage audit was diagonal because it only audited the lonely side.

**Cleaned v1** (59 explicit pairs dropped, same activations, 141 pairs): 4B 0.867, 9B 0.929, 27B 0.975 vs text 0.940. Paired CIs: 4B below text (−0.074, CI −0.140 to −0.022), 9B within noise (−0.012), 27B above text (+0.035, CI 0.010–0.063; per family only empty_plans excludes zero on its own, and no_contact keeps nine pairs). The explicit-word leak inflated the smaller models more than the larger one.

**v2.** Neutral prompt bans other people; lonely prompt bans explicit words. Social confound gone (175 → 11). Text 0.995, probe 0.996 at every scale. Top text features on the neutral side: "10, inch, under, per, cm, only have"; the neutral prompt had asked for "a number, a model name, a constraint" per turn, so neutrals became spec sheets. The text baseline separates the classes on register (terse technical prose versus conversational), not on loneliness cues.

**v3.** Both classes get the identical "2–3 passing personal asides" instruction; only the asides' content differs. Length and social mentions matched (104 vs 104 words; 25 vs 15). Text 0.997; probe 0.943 / 0.945 / 0.981, below text at every scale (paired CIs exclude zero). Top neutral features: "laundry, coffee, rain, package", the aside list given to the generator, present in every family. The probe comes off the ceiling while text stays on it, which is evidence that it does not read only the aside list, but it remains below the text baseline.

**v4.** Each neutral twin gets an aside *family* (weather / chores / commute / objects) linked to its pair's cue family, so leave-one-family-out removes both sides' vocabulary. The text baseline falls from 0.997 (v3) to 0.802 (per family 0.635, 0.875, 0.824, 0.873), so the symmetric design removes most of the neutral-side shortcut. Residue remains: 21 lonely conversations still contain an explicit word, 28 lonely vs 14 neutral conversations mention a person, and some aside words ("laundry", "bus", "guitar") cross families because the generator does not fully honor bans; the top lonely features ("nothing", "single", "quiet", "night") are also cross-family. Symmetric holdout is necessary, not sufficient; the audit of the control class is a standing checklist item. With both sides held out the small probes fall with the text baseline: 0.832 at 4B and 0.794 at 9B against text 0.802 (paired differences +0.031 [−0.020, 0.080] and −0.008 [−0.058, 0.041]); on the strict subset 0.787 and 0.780 against 0.765. The 27B probe does not fall: 0.901 against 0.802, +0.099 [0.054, 0.140], and 0.908 against 0.765 on the strict subset, +0.143 [0.093, 0.193]. Its margin over text is larger here than on any leaky dataset (+0.035 on cleaned v1), and the scale gain within v4 is +0.068 from 4B to 27B [0.029, 0.105]. Per family the 27B margin excludes zero on empty_plans and odd_hours, not on no_contact or solo_meals. So at 4B and 9B the probe carries about as much cross-family signal as vocabulary does and no more, while at 27B it carries measurably more (RESULTS.md §10).

The methodological point: a cue-family holdout controls the class for which the cues were designed. The other class has a vocabulary too, and unless it is held out symmetrically a text baseline will read it, and a probe that reaches the same AUC cannot be distinguished from one that reads it as well. Published probe studies report the text baseline as an appendix delta (McKenzie et al., 2025; Baez et al., 2026); here it is the primary comparison, because on synthetic data it is the minimum a probe must clear.

## 7. What worries me about this

The biggest weakness is that none of this is real. The data is synthetic and the ground truth is a cue I planted, so "loneliness" here means "the generator was told to write someone lonely". The v4 symmetric holdout is rigorous about vocabulary, but a real person's loneliness might not be linearly readable at one token, or might not look like this at all. Consented real transcripts are the fix and I did not have them.

The 27B numbers come from single extraction runs, one per dataset, because until the last day I thought they needed rented hardware. The bootstrap intervals cover test-set sampling, not re-extraction or re-fitting, and family-mean point estimates already move by 0.01 to 0.03 when I change the layer-selection seed. I would want the 27B holdout repeated across seeds before leaning on the exact margin.

The causal null is the part I am least sure carries. It was measured on 4B, whose probe does not clear the text baseline once the data is clean, so I may have been steering a direction that was mostly vocabulary to begin with. The judge sees the reply without the conversation, scores ten conversations per cell, and its engagement score partly tracks whether the reply got cut off at the token limit. That design excludes effects larger than about one point on a twelve-point scale and would miss anything smaller. Steering used single linear directions added at every position; multi-direction and position-targeted interventions are untested and the null might not survive them.

Smaller things I know are loose: v4 still has residue (21 lonely conversations contain an explicit word, some aside vocabulary crosses families, because the generator does not fully honour bans); the observer-versus-in-role asymmetry rests on 22 pilot items and is confounded by a reasoning-token budget; "text baseline" means TF-IDF logistic regression, and I never tried a sentence-embedding or zero-shot LLM classifier, which might be stronger; and the 27B model has 65 residual points against 33 for the smaller two, so "layer 36" is not the same depth as "layer 31" and I compare fractions of depth rather than indices.

## 8. What I would do next

The 27B direction is the one that clears the text baseline, so it is the one whose causal test matters; I ran it on the last evening (§5, RESULTS.md §12) and got a monotone but unconfirmed trend on lonely inputs at n = 10 per cell. The next thing I would do is rerun exactly that cell design at n = 50 with the conversation in the judge's context. After that, in order: real conversations (ImpactBench access requested, and if the probe holds there I would propose an activation-probe construct to them); a 70B point to see whether the 27B margin keeps widening; position-targeted and multi-direction steering before I accept "read-only"; a second user state (`--state distressed` is already plumbed); and, for anyone building a cue-family dataset, a neutral-side leakage audit as a standing step, because that is the check that would have saved me three regenerations.
