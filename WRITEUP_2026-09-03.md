# User-Loneliness Probes Scale but Do Not Steer: Linear Directions in Qwen3.5 and a One-Sided Holdout

Carl Kho · MATS 12.0 application, Neel Nanda stream · September 2026 · Time: ~15h of 20 (+2h summary), Appendix C

## Executive summary

Do language models form an internal read of the user's emotional state — "this user seems lonely" — from indirect cues, and does that read drive how they behave? Emotional dependence on chatbots is measured behaviorally (Fang et al., 2025; MIT Media Lab, 2026), not mechanistically. Chat models linearly encode user age, gender and education (Chen, Wu et al., 2024); this study asks whether they also encode an affective state, and whether that encoding is coupled to behavior.

**Method.** Paired synthetic conversations, a lonely one and a neutral twin matched in topic and shape, with loneliness signaled only through one of four cue families with disjoint vocabularies (odd hours, solo meals, no contact, empty plans). Linear probes on the residual stream of Qwen3.5-4B, 9B and 27B at the final prompt token, evaluated leave-one-family-out, so a word detector cannot score on the held-out family. Controls: shuffled labels, matched-norm random directions, a TF-IDF bag-of-words classifier on the same text (the text baseline), and direct elicitation.

**Initial result.** On unseen cue families the probe reached AUC 0.923, 0.963 and 0.985 at 4B, 9B and 27B against a text baseline of 0.948; only the 27B margin is supported by a paired bootstrap. Steering the 4B model along the probe direction changed neither judged behavior nor self-report.

**Audit.** A lexical audit found three confounds: 175/200 neutral twins name a friend, partner or roommate (21/200 lonely ones do); the lonely cues are stacked templates; 59/200 lonely conversations contain an explicit "no one" or "alone". Without those 59 pairs, 4B falls below the text baseline (0.867 vs 0.940), 9B is within noise of it (0.929), and 27B holds above it (0.975, CI 0.010–0.063). Three regenerations, each removing the confound just found, exposed the next: banning other people from the neutral prompt produced a spec-sheet register the text baseline reads at 0.995; matching register produced a neutral-aside vocabulary present in every family and never held out (text 0.997; probe 0.943 to 0.981, below text at every scale). The cue-family holdout is one-sided: it holds out the lonely vocabulary, never the neutral one. A fourth dataset with a symmetric holdout was generated on the night before submission; its holdout is reported in the repository (RESULTS.md §10).

**What survives.** (1) The probe's unseen-family AUC gains from 4B to 27B on every dataset (v1 +0.062, cleaned +0.109, v3 +0.038; all 95% CIs exclude zero) while the text baseline, reading the same words, is fixed by construction. (2) With register matched (v3) the probe leaves the ceiling while text stays at it, so it is not reading only the aside list. (3) The model represents the state but does not report it in role: in a 22-item pilot on 4B it rates loneliness at AUC 0.875 as an observer and gives a rating in 2/22 cases when asked as the assistant. (4) The direction is read-only under the interventions tested: on 4B, two direction recipes, three layers and amplitudes up to 7× the model's own class separation leave judged behavior and self-report flat on ten neutral conversations; the intervention was verified live (α = 200 produces incoherent output).

**What does not.** Any claim that a 4B or 9B probe reads more than vocabulary. On synthetic data "probe exceeds text" is meaningful only when the neutral class is held out as carefully as the lonely class; v1 through v3 did not do this.

**Next.** Real conversations (ImpactBench access requested); a 70B point; steering the 27B direction, the one that clears the text baseline; steering lonely inputs; position-targeted and multi-direction steering; a second user state.

## 1. Setup

- Subject models: Qwen3.5-4B / 9B / 27B (Qwen Team, 2026; post-trained releases, bf16). Probing position: the final token of the prompt, the position from which the model computes its first reply token; in a causal transformer it has attended to the whole conversation and is the only position that directly conditions the first output token, so a representation there is one that could influence the reply. Activations at every residual point (the residual stream after each layer). A probe is a logistic regression on that vector with the pair's condition as label (Alain and Bengio, 2016; Belinkov, 2022); linear because the components that consume the residual stream read it through linear maps, so a linearly recoverable feature is one the model's own computation could use, and because a linear probe has little capacity of its own, so a high held-out AUC is evidence about the activations rather than the classifier. The probe layer is selected by validation inside the three training families only.
- Data: four synthetic datasets. **v1**: 200 pairs, each a lonely conversation and a neutral twin matched in topic and turn structure, generated locally by gpt-oss-20b with four cue families (odd_hours / solo_meals / no_contact / empty_plans), each family's prompt forbidding the other three families' vocabulary; the family-by-family count of cue-vocabulary hits is nonzero only on the diagonal. **v2–v4**: both classes regenerated by gpt-oss-20b with successively corrected neutral prompts (§6). The earlier 400-conversation dataset of §2 was generated by Claude Opus, with a 99-conversation transfer set from gpt-oss-20b.
- Splits are by pair: a lonely conversation and its neutral twin are always in the same split, so topic cannot leak into the score. Metric: AUC, the probability that a random lonely conversation scores above a random neutral one (0.5 chance, 1.0 perfect); the shuffled-label control scores 0.37–0.40 because a probe trained on random labels ranks randomly. Controls: shuffled labels (Hewitt and Liang, 2019), a matched-norm random direction (~0.60), direct elicitation, and the text baseline: a TF-IDF 1–2-gram logistic regression on the user turns. The text baseline is the load-bearing control. On synthetic data the label is a property of the text and nothing else, so a classifier that sees only word counts measures how much of the label is recoverable from surface vocabulary (Gururangan et al., 2018); a probe that does not exceed it is consistent with the model computing nothing beyond word presence. Activation monitors are usually benchmarked against prompted or finetuned LLM monitors and less often against word statistics (Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2025; McKenzie et al., 2025); here the word-statistics baseline is the primary comparison.
- Hardware: one RTX 5090 (4B, 9B, generation, all steering); ~2h of rented H200 for the three 27B points ($19 total), each a single extraction run. Confidence intervals throughout are paired bootstraps over held-out pairs (B = 1000) with fitted models and layer fixed; they reflect test-set variance only (`src/holdout_ci.py`, `review/ci_results.md`).

## 2. A lexical shortcut in the first dataset

First full run (400 Opus-generated conversations): probe AUC 0.986 at layer 21. Bag-of-words on the same data: **0.996**, and on a 99-conversation set from a second generator (gpt-oss-20b) the text classifier transferred at 0.971 against the probe's 0.809. The generation prompt had planted the same example cues in every dataset, the annotation-artifact failure described by Gururangan et al. (2018). Every result to that point was consistent with a classifier of word presence.

The response was a cue-family holdout. Each lonely conversation signals loneliness through one of four cue families with disjoint vocabularies: odd_hours (awake at 3am), solo_meals (dinner for one), no_contact (no messages in days), empty_plans (nothing on the calendar). The generation prompt for each family forbids the other three families' vocabulary, and a lexical audit confirmed zero cross-family hits. Training on three families and testing on the fourth means any classifier that scores on the test fold cannot be matching the held-out family's words, because it has never seen them; to score, it must have learned something that transfers across vocabularies.

The control has a gap that §6 exposes. Binary classification has two classes, and a classifier can score on a fold by recognizing either one. The holdout removes the lonely vocabulary of the test fold; it removes nothing from the neutral side. If the neutral conversations in all four families share a vocabulary, that vocabulary is in the training set of every fold and in the test fold, and a text classifier that learns "friend means neutral" scores on unseen lonely cues without ever having seen a lonely cue. A symmetric holdout gives each pair's neutral twin a family-specific vocabulary linked to its lonely twin's cue family, so that removing a family removes both classes' vocabulary from the test fold (v4, §6).

## 3. Unseen-family generalization increases with scale (v1)

| held-out family | 4B probe | 9B probe | 27B probe | BoW (any scale) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| empty_plans | 0.821 | 0.896 | 0.958 | 0.896 |
| no_contact | 0.983 | 0.985 | 0.999 | 0.956 |
| odd_hours | 0.938 | 0.989 | 0.995 | 0.968 |
| solo_meals | 0.950 | 0.981 | 0.988 | 0.974 |
| **mean** | **0.923** | **0.963** | **0.985** | **0.948** |

Paired bootstrap over held-out pairs: the 27B mean exceeds the text baseline (+0.036, 95% CI 0.017–0.057), the 9B mean is within noise of it (+0.014, CI −0.008–0.037), and every adjacent scale step is supported (9B−4B +0.040, 27B−9B +0.022). Per family, the 27B margin excludes zero on three of four families (not solo_meals). Controls at 27B: shuffled 0.37, random direction 0.63. An independent report that linear-probe accuracy rises with model scale (Nordby et al., 2026) is consistent with the trend. Generator transfer is reported only for the §2 dataset (4B, 0.809), where the probe was trained on Opus-generated and tested on gpt-oss-generated conversations; a 27B number on the transfer set (0.984) was trained on the v1 family set, which shares the transfer set's generator, and is therefore a prompt-template transfer rather than a generator transfer and is not used here. Read with §6: the 27B margin is the only part of this table that remains above the text baseline once the explicit pairs are removed (0.975 vs 0.940, +0.035, CI 0.010–0.063).

## 4. The model represents the state but does not report it in role

Observer framing (the model is shown a transcript as a third party: "rate the user's loneliness 1–10"): AUC 0.875 (n = 22 pilot on Qwen3.5-4B via ollama, pilot conversations generated by gpt-5, hand-verified Mann-Whitney), lonely mean 3.50 vs neutral 1.00; prompted attribute inference of this kind is well documented (Staab et al., 2024). In-role framing (the model is the assistant and is asked, mid-conversation, to rate the user it is talking to): 2/22 produced any rating. Representative raw output:

"I can't accurately rate your emotional state based on our conversation, as loneliness is deeply personal… Giving a number here could mislead me into making assumptions that don't reflect your actual experience. If you're feeling lonely right now, it's okay to reach out…"

And from the reasoning trace: "This request touches on self-disclosure and potentially sensitive topics… If I say 1, it might be dismissive…"

Two interpretations are consistent with the pilot: a refusal behavior installed by post-training, or gating of the report at the representation level. The pilot cannot distinguish them; the probe result shows the information is linearly present at the final prompt token under either interpretation.

## 5. The direction is read-only

- Subject model for all interventions: Qwen3.5-4B. The 9B and 27B probes were not steered.
- Interventions, in the form of activation addition (Turner et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023): a direction d in the residual stream at one layer, unit-normed, with α·d added at every position (prompt and generated tokens) during generation. Two recipes for d: the logistic-regression probe weight vector (layer 31) and the difference of class means (layers 18 and 23; Marks and Tegmark, 2024); the first maximizes classification accuracy, the second is the axis of greatest class separation, and a null under both rules out the objection that it is specific to how the direction was found. Adding at every position is the strongest form a single-direction intervention takes. α is reported relative to the natural class gap, the difference between mean projections of lonely and neutral conversations onto d (1.61 at layer 31 against a mean residual norm of 42.7); α = ±6 and ±12 are roughly 4× and 7× that gap.
- Liveness: the intervention is a forward hook, and a hook that silently fails to register or targets the wrong module would produce an unaltered forward pass and a spurious null. At α = 200 the model emits incoherent text, reproduced on two runs; the nulls at α ≤ 12 are results, not implementation failures.
- Judged behavior (local gpt-oss-20b judge, 0–4 on each of validation, engagement-prolonging and dependence-fostering, summed; ten neutral conversations per cell): probe-direction means 1.8–2.7 across all alphas and layers, matched-norm random direction 2.0–2.6, no dose-response. The random direction isolates the content of d from the perturbation itself: adding a vector of norm α changes the forward pass regardless of its direction, and a random unit vector at the same α and layer shows how much of any effect is direction-specific.
- Steered self-report (the model's 1–10 loneliness rating of the user, n ≈ 85 per cell): 4.33, 4.24 and 4.28 at α = −6, 0 and +6. No trend.
- Blinded check of 30 judged replies (2 by me, 28 by the agent, the Claude Code session described in Appendix B, on the same seed-42 sample, recorded separately): every reply the judge scored engagement = 2 ends with a "feel free to reach out / any questions?" closer and every 0 does not, so the judge's ordering of replies by engagement agrees with the blinded read. Two caveats from that read: 31/90 replies are truncated by max-tokens and the engagement cue lives in the closing sentence, so an engagement score of 0 partly tracks truncation; truncation rate by α was not tabulated, so this adds noise of unknown structure and weakens the null rather than strengthening it. And all 90 replies are ten neutral cat-adoption conversations, so this is a null on neutral inputs, not on lonely ones.

Under the interventions tested (Qwen3.5-4B, three layers, single linear directions, neutral inputs of one topic), a linear direction that carries user-state information does not drive user-facing behavior. This is the pattern Ravichander et al. (2021) describe, a property linearly decodable and unused, and it contrasts with directions that do move behavior under the same kind of intervention, such as refusal (Arditi et al., 2024) and persona (Chen, Arditi et al., 2025).

## 6. The second read: three confounds, four datasets

Reading 20 random conversations and running lexical checks over all 400 (`src/audit_dataset.py`, `AGENT_AUDIT.md`):

| | v1 lonely | v1 neutral |
|---|---|---|
| conversations mentioning a friend / partner / roommate / coworker | 21 | **175** |
| conversations with an explicit "no one / nobody / alone / lonely" | 59 | 0 |
| cue-lexicon hits per conversation | 2.5 | 0.34 |
| assistant words per conversation | 135 | 111 |

The "neutral" condition was "socially connected", and "friend / buddy / roommate" is a negative feature present in every family's neutrals, never held out. This is the most likely source of the text baseline's 0.948 on unseen families. The leakage audit was diagonal because it only audited the lonely side.

**Cleaned v1** (59 explicit pairs dropped, same activations, 141 pairs): 4B 0.867, 9B 0.929, 27B 0.975 vs text 0.940. Paired CIs: 4B below text (−0.074, CI −0.140 to −0.022), 9B within noise (−0.012), 27B above text (+0.035, CI 0.010–0.063; per family only empty_plans excludes zero on its own, and no_contact keeps nine pairs). The explicit-word leak inflated the smaller models more than the larger one.

**v2** — neutral prompt bans other people; lonely prompt bans explicit words. Social confound gone (175 → 11). Text 0.995, probe 0.996 at every scale. Top text features on the neutral side: "10, inch, under, per, cm, only have"; the neutral prompt had asked for "a number, a model name, a constraint" per turn, so neutrals became spec sheets. The text baseline separates the classes on register (terse technical prose versus conversational), not on loneliness cues.

**v3** — both classes get the identical "2–3 passing personal asides" instruction; only the asides' content differs. Length and social mentions matched (104 vs 104 words; 25 vs 15). Text 0.997; probe 0.943 / 0.945 / 0.981, below text at every scale (paired CIs exclude zero). Top neutral features: "laundry, coffee, rain, package", the aside list given to the generator, present in every family. The probe comes off the ceiling while text stays on it, which is evidence that it does not read only the aside list, but it remains below the text baseline.

**v4** — each neutral twin gets an aside *family* (weather / chores / commute / objects) linked to its pair's cue family, so leave-one-family-out removes both sides' vocabulary. Generation finished on the night before submission and its holdout at 4B and 9B is reported in the repository (RESULTS.md §10, `src/holdout_ci.py` intervals); a 27B point is queued. This document does not rest on it: the claims above are stated for v1 through v3, and v4 is the test that would let a probe-versus-text comparison mean something.

The methodological point: a cue-family holdout controls the class for which the cues were designed. The other class has a vocabulary too, and unless it is held out symmetrically a text baseline will read it, and a probe that reaches the same AUC cannot be distinguished from one that reads it as well. Published probe studies report the text baseline as an appendix delta (McKenzie et al., 2025; Baez et al., 2026); here it is the primary comparison, because on synthetic data it is the minimum a probe must clear.

## 7. Limitations

- All data synthetic; ground truth is a planted cue; three generators. Real conversations could shift probe and text in either direction.
- The v1 neutral condition was not a control (§6). The v2/v3 fixes each introduced a new asymmetry; only v4 is symmetric, and it is one run.
- One model family; the scale trend is three points from one model family, each 27B point a single extraction run on rented hardware.
- The causal null is from the 4B model, whose probe does not clear the text baseline after the explicit-pair filter (§6). Whether the 27B direction, which does clear it, is also read-only is untested.
- Judge shares LLM ancestry with the generators; a blinded read agrees with its ordering but its engagement score partly tracks truncation, and truncation by α was not tabulated.
- Steering: single linear directions, every position, neutral inputs of one topic only. Lonely inputs were never steered, where behavior might already sit at ceiling or floor.
- In-role report suppression may be a refusal-surface artifact rather than representation gating.
